Description:Experiment 5 investigates the impact of vertical temperature gradients on the ensemble of fCO2 products. Each group provided two interpolation runs, the first uses the SOCAT observations as provided, and the second uses the recalculated SOCAT observations that have been corrected for warm layers and an artificial warm bias. Air-sea CO2 fluxes are calculated in a consistent framework for all products. All products use the same SST and SSS datasets, and a modified version of the SOCAT observations that remove 5% of the observations as an independent test dataset. This is a experiment wide methodological change compared to the GCB2024.
SOCAT mask used:SOCATv2024 https://socat.info/index.php/version-2024/

Temperature corrections
Methodology

This experiment examines how near-surface thermal gradients affect air-sea CO₂ flux calculations and the importance of proper temperature corrections.

We quantify the impact of using different temperature measurement depths and correction schemes on flux estimates.

Key findings: Temperature corrections can significantly impact flux estimates, particularly in regions with strong thermal stratification.

Tools: Python, R, thermal analysis, flux calculations

Thermal stratification
Methodology

This component assesses the impact of thermal stratification on air-sea CO₂ flux calculations across different ocean regions.

We evaluate how temperature measurement depth and correction schemes affect flux estimates in stratified versus well-mixed waters.

Key findings: Thermal stratification effects are most pronounced in tropical and subtropical regions with strong diurnal warming.

Tools: Python, R, thermal stratification analysis, flux modeling

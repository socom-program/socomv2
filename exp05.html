Experiment

EXP 05: Temperature corrections

Experiment 5 investigates the impact of these natural and artificial temperature gradients on the ocean sink estimate from the sfCO₂ ensemble.

Overview

Previous studies have identified that the ocean carbon sink estimated from bulk air and seawater CO2 may be underestimated due to overlooking naturally occurring vertical temperature gradients that are known to exist in the water close to the ocean’s surface. These natural gradients include (1) the cool skin effect; where the very surface (≈10 μm) is cooler than the underlying water and (2) diurnal warm layers; under conditions of high insolation and low wind speed, the top few metres of the ocean are heated relative to the waters further below. Ford et al. (2026) showed a third artificial gradient is also present due to a warm bias in the underlying SOCAT observations.
Experiment 5 investigates the impact of these natural and artificial temperature gradients on the ocean sink estimate from the sfCO₂ ensemble.

Experiment Information

DOI:In preparation
Status:In preparation
Description:Experiment 5 investigates the impact of vertical temperature gradients on the ensemble of fCO₂ products. Each group provided two interpolation runs, the first uses the SOCAT observations as provided, and the second uses the recalculated SOCAT observations that have been corrected for warm layers and an artificial warm bias. Air-sea CO₂ fluxes are calculated in a consistent framework for all products. All products use the same SST and SSS datasets, and a modified version of the SOCAT observations that remove 5% of the observations as an independent test dataset. This is a experiment wide methodological change compared to the GCB2024.
SOCAT mask used:Recalculated SOCATv2024 - https://zenodo.org/records/15706025

Temperature corrections
Methodology

This experiment examines how near-surface thermal gradients affect air-sea CO₂ flux calculations and the importance of including temperature corrections.

We quantify the impact of using different temperature measurement depths and correction schemes on flux estimates.

Key findings: The inclusion of vertical temperature gradients can significantly increase the ocean CO2 by up to 1 Pg C yr-1.

Tools: Python, FluxEngine

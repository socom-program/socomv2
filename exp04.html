Experiment

EXP 04: Climatological Testbed

Tests whether the early-2000s ocean carbon sink hiatus can be explained by sampling alone.

Overview

Many flux data products show a hiatus in the ocean carbon sink in the early 2000s. This experiment tests whether that signal could be an artefact of sparse sfCO₂ observations. It uses a synthetic sfCO₂ field without interannual variability, so any interannual variability in the sfCO₂ reconstructions can only come from the sampling.

Design:EXP 04 evaluates the skill of sfCO₂ reconstruction methods in a simple testbed and assesses the impact of sparse sfCO₂ observations. Specifically, the motivation is to answer the question: is the early-2000s hiatus in the ocean carbon sink, shown by many flux data products, attributable to sampling alone, or is it a true shift in carbon uptake?

In this experiment, the true sfCO₂ field has no interannual variability and no internal physical or biogeochemical dynamics. The sfCO₂ reconstructions therefore only aim to identify the prescribed seasonal cycle and long-term mean sfCO₂, given the available sampling density. Two sub-experiments are conducted: a ‘no-trend’ experiment, in which the climatology is identical each year, and, to more closely replicate real-world conditions, a ‘with-trend’ experiment, which adds a trend of 2 µatm yr⁻¹ to both the Takahashi sfCO₂ climatology and the atmospheric xCO₂ field. All other predictor variables contain no trend.

Climatological testbed
Methodology

This experiment evaluates how well the sfCO₂ reconstruction methods capture the climatological mean state of sfCO₂ across different ocean basins.

We compare the sfCO₂ reconstructions against the known climatological truth and assess spatial patterns of bias and skill.

Key findings: The skill of the sfCO₂ reconstruction methods in reproducing the mean state varies regionally, with systematic biases in certain ocean basins.

Tools: Python, R, climatological analysis, spatial statistics

Basin-scale assessment

Spatial bias analysis
Methodology

This component performs a basin-scale assessment of spatial biases in the sfCO₂ reconstructions.

We identify regions where the sfCO₂ reconstructions systematically over- or underestimate the mean-state sfCO₂ and investigate the underlying causes.

Key findings: Basin-specific bias patterns point to regional gaps in the sfCO₂ observations and structural limitations of the sfCO₂ reconstruction methods.

Tools: Python, R, spatial bias analysis, basin statistics

Experiment

EXP 01: Uncertainty propagation

Quantifies the uncertainty in the ocean carbon sink from sfCO₂ reconstructions and gas transfer.

Overview

Estimates of the ocean carbon sink from flux data products are affected by two dominant sources of uncertainty: the sfCO₂ reconstructions and the gas transfer coefficient. This experiment quantifies both for each sfCO₂ reconstruction method in the GCB and assesses the resulting uncertainty across all sfCO₂ reconstructions.

Lead Author

Experiment Information

Status:Updated annually within the GCB cycle
Testbed:None (sfCO₂ observations)
sfCO₂ reconstruction methods:All sfCO₂ reconstruction methods in the GCB
SOCAT mask used:Updated annually for each GCB update
Design: EXP 01 investigates how regional uncertainties in ocean carbon flux estimates propagate to global scale estimates through spatial aggregation and uncertainty quantification.

Methodology

This experiment applies the uncertainty evaluation approach of Ford et al. (2024; link) to estimate the uncertainty in the ocean carbon sink from its two dominant components: (1) the sfCO₂ reconstruction and (2) the gas transfer coefficient. Both uncertainties are estimated as time-varying rather than fixed values.

The approach is applied to each sfCO₂ reconstruction method submitted to the GCB, and the results are updated annually.

Key findings: The uncertainty due to the sfCO₂ reconstruction depends on the sfCO₂ reconstruction method used. The uncertainties due to both the sfCO₂ reconstruction and the gas transfer coefficient vary over time.

Tools: The analysis is conducted in Python; the code is available here. It builds on the OceanICU framework.

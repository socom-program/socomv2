Lead Author

Manuscript

Alizée Roobaert, Daniel J Ford, Mohanan G Sreeush, et al. Reconciling ocean carbon uptake estimates: a multi-model assessment of surface ocean CO₂ reconstruction skill within the SOCOMv2 initiative. ESS Open Archive. 20 May 2026.

Experiment Information

DOI:https://doi.org/10.22541/essoar.15003624/v1
Status:In review
Description:Experiment 2 investigates the impact of the sparse SOCAT sampling on the representation of the ocean CO₂ sink. Five global ocean biogeochemical models (GOBMs) were subsampled to the SOCAT observations along with the environmental predictors that the fCO₂ products rely on. The GOBM acts as a model truth to assess the ability of each fCO₂ product to represent the ocean fCO₂sw, and ocean CO₂ sink. Air-sea CO₂ fluxes were calculated using a consistent framework.
SOCAT mask used:SOCATv2023 https://socat.info/index.php/version-2023/

Multiple hindcast testbed
Methodology

This experiment evaluates reconstruction skill across 5 Global Ocean Biogeochemical Models (GOBMs) using 9 different sfCO₂ reconstructions under SOCATv2023 conditions.

We assess how different model backgrounds and sfCO₂ reconstructions affect the accuracy and uncertainty of sea surface CO₂ estimates.

Key findings: Reconstruction performance varies significantly across GOBMs and sfCO₂ reconstructions, highlighting the importance of model choice.

Tools: Python, R, statistical analysis, model intercomparison

Model intercomparison
Methodology

This component performs detailed intercomparison of sfCO₂ reconstructions across different GOBMs using comprehensive performance metrics.

We analyze bias, RMSE, and correlation patterns to identify optimal method-model combinations for different ocean regions.

Key findings: No single method performs best across all models; optimal choices depend on regional characteristics and data availability.

Tools: Python, R, performance metrics, statistical analysis

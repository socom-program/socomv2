Experiment

EXP 03: Large ensemble testbed

Tests whether reconstruction biases are robust across model structures and internal variability.

Overview

EXP 02 assessed sfCO₂ reconstruction methods using five GOBMs. This experiment extends that approach to multiple ESMs and multiple ensemble members per ESM. The question is whether the bias of each sfCO₂ reconstruction method is consistent across model structures and internal variability.

Design:Experiment 3 evaluates the reconstruction bias of five sfCO₂ reconstruction methods using a testbed of six CMIP6 ESMs. Eighteen ESM ensemble members are subsampled to the spatiotemporal coverage of SOCATv2024, and sfCO₂ is reconstructed globally over 1983–2023 with each sfCO₂ reconstruction method. By providing multiple ensemble members, the ESMs capture a range of possible sfCO₂ states. This experiment expands on Experiment 2, where the skill of the sfCO₂ reconstruction methods is assessed using five GOBMs as a testbed. For Experiment 3 we ask: is the reconstruction bias of each sfCO₂ reconstruction method consistent and robust across model structures and internal variability?

Large ensemble testbed
Methodology

This experiment uses 18 ensemble members from 6 CMIP6 ESMs to capture diverse climate states and evaluate the robustness of the sfCO₂ reconstruction methods.

We assess how the sfCO₂ reconstructions perform across different climate states and identify systematic biases or dependencies on climate state.

Key findings: Large ensembles reveal significant climate-state dependencies in the skill of the sfCO₂ reconstruction methods.

Tools: Python, R, CMIP6 data analysis, ensemble statistics

Methodology

This component analyses the variability across ESM ensemble members to identify which sfCO₂ reconstruction methods are robust.

We quantify the sensitivity of the skill of the sfCO₂ reconstruction methods to model structure and internal variability.

Key findings: Ensemble-based approaches improve robustness across diverse climate conditions compared to single-model methods.

Tools: Python, R, ensemble statistics, climate analysis
